TechBuffalo Tech Design Challenge

Using AI tools to solve real life problems.

About the Project

Mission: Ignite is a nonprofit organization that seeks to spread computer literacy and reduce electronic waste across the Buffalo and wider WNY area. They reduce e-waste by recycling older systems, and they use these systems to hold computer literacy classes for disadvantaged youths throughout various sites in the area. They begin and end their classes with literacy tests for the kids, to find out what they started out knowing, and what they learned. A great idea, however, this leads to a big problem, a data problem. Data from all of their sites are aggregated into fiendish, unorganized spreadsheets that are rife with inconsistencies and are near unusable for analysis. The task: create something that will fix it. Myself, along with my team of 4 others set out to solve this within a time span of two days.

The end product my team devised was a software tool that took the raw spreadsheet data, and essentially reorganized it, along with providing useful analysis metrics such as performance by site, ratings for each instructor, strongest topics, etc. We took raw data and considered the goal of Mission: Ignite to bring a solution that was easy to use, scalable, and low cost. What we used AI for was the actual creation of the tool - coding from scratch would take too long, and not everyone on the team was able to use technology like that. What everyone on the team contributed was their creativity and ideas.

Thanks to all our hard work we managed to win second place out of a total of 5 teams competing (about 30 people). The reason we finished second instead of first place was because the winning team had a better pitch of their solution. This really drove home the point that you need to be able to have the skills to communicate your ideas and product to end users. Clients shouldn't have to know exactly how something works just to use it.

My Role

As for my own contributions to the project, I decided to approach this problem from the perspective of a customer for an accounting information system. I asked myself things like: "what kind of information would I prioritize to make future decisions?" and "if I wanted to gauge performance and had to make shareholder reports, what data would I need to know?" Generally speaking, answers to these questions would be things like being able to easily see trends over time, as well as the ability to filter items by location, topic, instructor, etc. I would need to easily distinguish weak areas that I could devote more manpower to, as well as have this solution be scalable for future operations. As a result, I implemented a person number system for each student in order to add scalability (what if I wanted to use the data for other analysis not covered by our team's tool? A person number adds cross-compatibility), as well as a tag system that tagged each data entry with things like location and instructor.

What I Learned

While yes, I did pick up some technical skills during this project like AI use, I think the most important thing to walk away with is the team coordination and presentation aspect. I wasn't the only one who had ideas to implement in our project, so combining and compromising between team members was key to our success. Not only did we have to create something, but we had to pitch it as well. I think that the pitch can be almost as important as the product itself - you can have the most optimal solution but if the client can't understand how it's optional then they wont adopt it. I think these are the real skills that will follow me into any workplace, especially in a field like accounting where you have to work with different people and be able to communicate your ideas effectively.
